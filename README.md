    <h1>Google Ads for Auto Detailing: Campaign Structure, Keywords, and Conversion Tracking</h1>

    <p>Your ads are getting clicks. Your booking diary still has gaps. And somehow, the enquiries coming through are for a quick wash when you wanted ceramic coating jobs.</p>

    <p><a href="https://noahfordetailers.com/google-ads-for-car-detailers/" target="_blank" rel="noopener">Google Ads</a> for auto detailing needs a clear connection between the service someone searches for, the page they land on, and the action you measure. If you already run Meta Ads, this gives you another way to reach potential customers: when they actively search for a detailer. Here’s how to build that connection without making your account unnecessarily complicated.</p>

    <h2>Build your Google Ads for auto detailing around specific services</h2>

    <p>Start with the work you want more of. A shop focused on ceramic coating, paint correction, and paint protection film should build its campaigns around those services.</p>

    <p>For a modest starting budget, I’d use one local Search campaign with separate service-based ad groups. Each group should connect closely related keywords, relevant ads, and a dedicated landing page. Google’s account structure supports this approach: campaigns control budgets and location settings, while ad groups organise related ads and keywords.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/6372655?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[1]</a></p>

    <p>Replace Dublin with your actual location. Split services into separate campaigns when they need independent budgets or different targeting; if funds are tight, start with your strongest service instead of promoting everything at once.</p>

    <h2>Keep location targeting close to your customers</h2>

    <p>Target the area customers realistically travel from, or the area your mobile team serves. For a local shop, consider “Presence: People in or regularly in your targeted locations” so targeting focuses on people likely to be there. Google’s broader default also includes people showing interest in the location.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9376662?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[2]</a></p>

    <h2>Choose keywords that suggest someone wants to book</h2>

    <p>“Ceramic coating installer near me” and “how to apply ceramic coating” describe different needs. For a campaign selling installation, I’d prioritise searches naming the service, a local provider, or the location.</p>

    <p>Exact and phrase match offer a controlled starting point, but neither guarantees literal wording. Exact match can include searches with the same meaning or intent; phrase match can include searches containing the keyword’s meaning. Broad match reaches further, and Google recommends pairing it with Smart Bidding.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7478529?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[3]</a></p>

    <p>Check the search terms report to see the actual queries Google makes available, then assess whether those people wanted your service. Review it frequently after launch and regularly once the campaign settles.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2472708?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[4]</a></p>

    <h2>Add negatives without blocking potential customers</h2>

    <p>If you only sell detailing services, negative keyword candidates include “jobs,” “training course,” “DIY kit,” and “wholesale supplies.” Choose them based on your actual offer and the searches appearing in your account.</p>

    <p>Be careful with “price” and “cost”: someone researching ceramic coating prices may be a serious buyer. Negative match types also behave differently from positive keywords, so check the scope of each exclusion and add relevant singular or plural forms where needed.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2453972?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[5]</a></p>

    <h2>Give each ad a landing page that answers the search</h2>

    <p>A ceramic coating ad should lead to a page explaining your coating service. Show completed work, genuine customer reviews, what the package includes, your location, and a clear way to request a quote.</p>

    <p>Possible headlines include “Ceramic Coating in Dublin” and “Get Your Coating Quote.” Use claims you can substantiate, and explain what affects pricing if every vehicle needs an individual assessment.</p>

    <p>Keep the form focused on information needed to respond: contact details, vehicle, and service interest. Test it on your phone, including the confirmation message. A beautiful page with a broken submit button is an expensive brochure.</p>

    <h2>Set up Google Ads for auto detailing to measure real enquiries</h2>

    <p>Before launch, decide which actions count as leads. A visitor opening WhatsApp has shown interest, but you still need to know whether they sent a message.</p>

    <p>For standard goals, primary actions inform bidding when the campaign uses their goal; secondary actions are generally for observation. Keep engagement actions out of custom bidding goals, because those can use secondary actions for bidding too.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/11461796?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[6]</a></p>

    <h3>Track the successful submission</h3>

    <p>Install the Google tag across your site, directly or through Google Tag Manager, then create a conversion for a successfully completed form. Trigger it from a confirmed submission event, or use a dedicated thank-you page reached after success; a general visit to your domain does not establish that someone enquired.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16560108?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[7]</a><a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9148089?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[8]</a></p>

    <p>Check tagging across the full journey, including any booking subdomain. If the same enquiry is measured through both a native Google Ads conversion and a GA4 import, choose one as the primary bidding signal to avoid counting the same outcome twice.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/16560108?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[7]</a><a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/9148089?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[8]</a></p>

    <p>For lead actions, “One” is usually the appropriate counting setting. It limits counting to one conversion per ad click for that action; it does not deduplicate separate conversion actions.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/3438531?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[9]</a></p>

    <h2>Measure calls and test the whole journey</h2>

    <p>Where supported, Google call reporting uses forwarding numbers to measure calls from ads. Choose a duration threshold informed by genuine enquiries, and remember that a long call can still be irrelevant; website calls need their own tracking setup.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/2454052?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[10]</a></p>

    <p>Use Tag Assistant to test a successful form submission and inspect the conversion tag. Also test an unsuccessful submission and a page refresh to catch accidental triggers or duplicate events.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/10989978?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[11]</a></p>

    <h2>Let bookings guide your budget</h2>

    <p>Choose conversion-focused bidding only after verifying what your account measures. Maximising conversions without a target CPA aims to generate the most conversions within your budget, so a wrongly configured conversion can steer spending towards the wrong action.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/7381968?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[12]</a></p>

    <p>Judge results against your booking records. In a hypothetical example, €400 spent generating ten leads and two bookings means €40 per lead and €200 in ad spend per booking. Whether that works depends on job value, delivery costs, and profit margin.</p>

    <p>As tracking matures, use offline conversion imports to report qualified leads or completed sales back to Google. Enhanced conversions for leads can help match those outcomes to advertising interactions using appropriately collected customer data.<a href="https://support.google.com/google-ads/answer/15713840?hl=en" class="sup-link" target="_blank" rel="nofollow noopener">[13]</a></p>

    <h2>Give Google Ads and Meta Ads clear roles</h2>

    <p>Google Search can put your offer in front of someone searching for a local service. With Meta Ads, you can also introduce that service through demonstrations, customer stories, and examples of your work.</p>

    <p>Compare them using qualified enquiries, bookings, and acquisition costs. A cheap lead loses its appeal when nobody books.</p>

    <h2>Start with one service and a working booking journey</h2>

    <p>Before increasing spend, choose one priority service, check its search terms, improve its landing page, and submit a test enquiry yourself. Then compare the leads in Google Ads with the conversations your team actually handled.</p>
